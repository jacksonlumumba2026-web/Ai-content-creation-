## Narration
Njoroge runs a hardware shop in Murang'a. It's a busy Saturday when his phone rings. Hello, this is
Safaricom. There's a problem with your M-Pesa account. We've sent you a code, please read it to us so we can
fix it. Njoroge stops. The number on his screen is an ordinary mobile number. Here's what Safaricom itself
says. Rule one: Safaricom only calls customers from one number, zero seven two two, zero zero zero, zero zero
zero. Any other number saying it's Safaricom is not Safaricom. Rule two: even on that line, they will never
ask for your M-Pesa PIN or a code sent to your phone. Scammers can fake caller ID too, so the PIN rule always
wins. Rule three: forward the scammer's number by SMS to three three three. It's free. So Njoroge hangs up,
and forwards the number. Share this with someone who keeps money on M-Pesa.
