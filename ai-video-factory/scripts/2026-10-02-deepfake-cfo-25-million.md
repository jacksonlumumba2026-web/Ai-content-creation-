## Narration
A finance worker in Hong Kong gets a message from his company's chief financial officer. It asks for a secret
transaction. He's suspicious. So he joins a video call to check. On the screen: the CFO and several colleagues.
Their faces look right. Their voices sound right. So he follows the instructions and makes fifteen transfers,
worth about twenty-five million dollars. Only later, when he checks with head office, does he learn the truth.
Everyone on that call was a deepfake. This really happened, at the engineering firm Arup, in 2024. And today,
a voice can be cloned from as little as three seconds of audio. So protect your business. Number one: never
pay based on a call or video alone. Number two: call back on a number you already know. Number three: agree
on a secret code word with your team. Would your team spot the fake? Share this with them.
